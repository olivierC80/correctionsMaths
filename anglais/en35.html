📗 Frankenstein; or, The Modern Prometheus (Mary Shelley, 1818)

🧭 Résumé rapide

Victor Frankenstein, jeune savant idéaliste, veut percer le secret de la vie. Il crée un être vivant à partir de cadavres, mais, horrifié par son apparence, il l’abandonne. Rejeté et persécuté, la créature devient monstrueuse — non par nature, mais par solitude et désespoir. Le roman explore la responsabilité du créateur, les limites de la science, et la quête d’humanité.

🧠 T1 – Technology & Society

Angle : L’ambition de dominer la nature et ses conséquences sociales.

Victor Frankenstein represents the modern scientist who seeks progress without thinking of moral consequences. Shelley warns against blind faith in science — a theme still relevant in AI and genetic engineering.

Phrase-type : Mary Shelley shows that science without empathy leads to destruction, not enlightenment.

⚙️ T8 – Science & Ethics

Angle : Science sans conscience, responsabilité du savant.

Victor creates life but refuses to care for it — the real monstrosity is not the creature, but his lack of responsibility. The novel raises timeless ethical questions: Should we do everything we can do? What happens when creation escapes control?

Phrase-type : Shelley’s novel is the first warning against the moral blindness of scientific ambition.

🗣️ T7 – Freedom of Speech / Thought

Angle : L’exclusion de la différence et le droit d’exister.

The creature learns to speak, to think, and to love — but society rejects him for being “different.” His voice is powerful: he becomes a symbol for all the silenced, the marginalized, or the misunderstood.

Phrase-type : By giving the monster a voice, Shelley gives humanity to the inhuman — and questions who the real monster is.

💰 T10 – Inequality

Angle : Rejet, marginalisation et injustice.

The creature is intelligent and sensitive, yet condemned to loneliness because of his appearance. Shelley exposes how societies exclude those who don’t fit their norms — a metaphor for social and moral inequality.

Phrase-type : The monster is not born evil — he becomes evil because he’s excluded.

🎨 T14 – Art & Culture

Angle : Une œuvre romantique et gothique sur la condition humaine.

Frankenstein combines science, philosophy, and emotion. It’s the first true “modern myth” — the scientist as creator and destroyer. The subtitle The Modern Prometheus links Victor to the Greek titan who stole fire from the gods to give it to mankind — punished for his ambition.

Phrase-type : Shelley turns the myth of Prometheus into a meditation on human pride and the cost of knowledge.

💬 Citations clés

  • “You are my creator, but I am your master — obey!”
  • “I ought to be thy Adam, but I am rather the fallen angel.”
  • “Nothing is so painful to the human mind as a great and sudden change.”

Usage oral : Ces citations permettent d’aborder la relation créateur-créature, la responsabilité morale et la quête d’identité.

💡 À retenir pour impressionner le jury

“Frankenstein is not about a monster — it’s about what happens when humanity forgets what makes it human.”
