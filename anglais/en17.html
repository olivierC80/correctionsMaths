🧩 T13 – American Society

The land of dreams — or of contradictions?

🔹 Idée générale

Les États-Unis se présentent comme le pays de la liberté, des opportunités et du rêve américain.
Mais derrière cet idéal se cachent de fortes inégalités sociales, raciales et culturelles.
L’enjeu : comment concilier le mythe de l’égalité avec la réalité des fractures américaines ?

🔹 Idées clés à développer

  1. The American Dream : success through work and merit.
  2. Social inequalities : healthcare, education, wealth gap.
  3. Racial tensions : from slavery to Black Lives Matter.
  4. Gun culture and violence : freedom vs security.
  5. Polarization : divided politics, culture wars, and identity conflicts.

🔹 Citations intégrées dans des phrases

  • The American Declaration promises that “all men are created equal,” yet, as history shows, equality has always been more of an ideal than a reality.
  • Martin Luther King Jr. dreamed of a nation where people would “not be judged by the color of their skin but by the content of their character” — a dream still unfulfilled today.
  • Barack Obama once said, “We are not as divided as our politics suggest,” though the rise of polarization has proved him partly wrong.
  • As writer James Baldwin noted, “Not everything that is faced can be changed, but nothing can be changed until it is faced.”
  • The Second Amendment gives Americans “the right to bear arms,” but, as many tragedies show, this right often turns into a curse.

🔹 Exemples de phrases prêtes à placer

  • America is both a dream and a warning — a land of opportunity and inequality.
  • The idea of meritocracy is inspiring, but social mobility in the US is lower than in many European countries.
  • Gun violence has become part of the national routine — freedom sometimes kills freedom.
  • Racial issues are not a chapter of history but a daily struggle.
  • The American Dream still exists — but it now depends more on your ZIP code than your talent.

🔹 Exemple de structure orale

“The United States calls itself the land of freedom, but also struggles with deep divisions.
As the Declaration said, ‘all men are created equal,’ yet racial injustice and economic inequality persist.
Martin Luther King’s dream remains partly unrealized, while gun violence and polarization show how fragile American unity has become.
The American Dream still inspires the world — but it’s no longer guaranteed by birth, only by luck and resilience.”
