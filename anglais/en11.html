🧩 T7 – Freedom of Speech

The right to speak — and the duty to think

🔹 Idée générale

La liberté d’expression est un pilier des démocraties modernes : elle garantit la critique, la créativité et le progrès.
Mais elle se heurte à des limites : discours de haine, désinformation, censure ou autocensure.
L’enjeu actuel : comment défendre la liberté sans détruire le respect ?

🔹 Idées clés à développer

  1. Foundations : Enlightenment thinkers (Voltaire, John Stuart Mill), First Amendment in the US.
  2. Freedom vs Responsibility : expressing opinions vs spreading hate or lies.
  3. Modern challenges : social media, fake news, cancel culture, disinformation.
  4. Censorship and satire : from Charlie Hebdo to campus debates.
  5. Cultural differences : freedom in Western vs authoritarian countries.

🔹 Citations intégrées dans des phrases

  • As Voltaire famously said, “I disapprove of what you say, but I will defend to the death your right to say it” — a sentence that still defines the spirit of democracy today.
  • John Stuart Mill warned that “the peculiar evil of silencing the expression of an opinion is that it robs the human race” — because truth only survives through debate.
  • Orwell showed in 1984 that when language is controlled, thought itself becomes impossible.
  • After the Charlie Hebdo attacks, France was reminded that freedom of speech can be dangerous, yet it remains the ultimate sign of a free society.
  • As journalist Maria Ressa put it, “Without facts, you can’t have truth. Without truth, you can’t have trust. Without trust, we have no shared reality.”

🔹 Exemples de phrases prêtes à placer

  • Freedom of speech is not about saying what we like, but about allowing others to say what we dislike.
  • Social media have turned free expression into a marketplace of outrage.
  • Censorship is not always imposed — it can also come from fear of being judged or “cancelled.”
  • The line between offence and expression is not fixed; it evolves with culture and context.
  • In authoritarian regimes, silence is imposed; in democracies, it is sometimes chosen.

🔹 Exemple de structure orale

“Freedom of speech is easy to defend when we agree with the speaker — the real test is when we don’t.
As Voltaire said, tolerance means defending even ideas we reject.
Yet today, freedom faces new threats: censorship by algorithms, outrage on social media, and the fear of being ‘cancelled’.
In the end, free speech is not just a right — it’s a responsibility to listen.”
