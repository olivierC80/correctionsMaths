🧩 T11 – Media & Truth

Information or manipulation?

🔹 Idée générale

Les médias ont pour mission d’informer, d’éduquer et de défendre la démocratie.
Mais à l’ère des réseaux sociaux, la frontière entre information, opinion et manipulation devient floue.
L’enjeu : comment préserver la vérité dans un monde saturé de données et de mensonges ?

🔹 Idées clés à développer

  1. The power of the media : shaping public opinion, setting the agenda.
  2. Fake news and disinformation : the post-truth era.
  3. Freedom of the press vs propaganda : censorship, political pressure, manipulation.
  4. Social media and algorithms : echo chambers, polarization.
  5. The role of journalism : ethics, fact-checking, independence.

🔹 Citations intégrées dans des phrases

  • As Orwell warned in 1984, “Who controls the past controls the future; who controls the present controls the past” — a chilling reminder of how truth can be rewritten by those in power.
  • Hannah Arendt argued that when facts lose their meaning, “people can be made to believe the most fantastic statements one day, and trust the opposite the next.”
  • Maria Ressa said, “Without facts, you can’t have truth. Without truth, you can’t have trust” — a perfect summary of today’s crisis of information.
  • The Watergate affair proved that journalism can still hold the powerful accountable — when it remains free and courageous.
  • In the film Don’t Look Up, the media’s obsession with entertainment turns catastrophe into comedy — a satire of our post-truth world.

🔹 Exemples de phrases prêtes à placer

  • We don’t live in an age of lies, but in an age of confusion.
  • Truth has become a product — and outrage sells better than facts.
  • Social networks give everyone a voice, but they also give lies an echo.
  • Freedom of the press is not guaranteed by law alone — it depends on the courage to speak the truth.
  • In a world of algorithms, truth needs human judgment.
