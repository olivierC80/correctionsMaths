🧩 T5 – Work & Productivity

Balancing ambition, well-being, and meaning

🔹 Idée générale

Le travail est à la fois source d’épanouissement et de contrainte.
Il structure nos vies, mais il interroge aussi notre rapport au temps, au progrès et au bonheur.
L’enjeu moderne : travailler mieux, pas forcément plus.

🔹 Idées clés à développer

  1. The value of work : identity, dignity, purpose.
  2. The dark side of productivity : stress, burnout, alienation.
  3. Technological change : automation, AI, and the future of employment.
  4. Work–life balance : flexibility, remote work, and well-being.
  5. Ethics and meaning : should work define who we are?

🔹 Citations intégrées dans des phrases

  • As Karl Marx observed, when work turns into mere survival, people become “alienated from their own humanity.”
  • Max Weber believed that the Protestant ethic made hard work a moral obligation — an idea that still shapes Western societies today.
  • In Chaplin’s Modern Times, the worker literally becomes part of the machine, a timeless symbol of industrial dehumanization.
  • “Choose a job you love,” Confucius allegedly said, “and you will never have to work a day in your life” — a beautiful idea, though rarely true for most people.
  • The Covid pandemic reminded us, as many discovered through remote work, that productivity without meaning is just another form of slavery.

🔹 Exemples de phrases prêtes à placer

  • Work gives meaning to life, but it can also take life away if it becomes obsessive.
  • Today’s society measures success by output rather than fulfillment.
  • AI and automation may replace tasks, but not human creativity or empathy.
  • The modern worker is connected all the time — yet often feels more isolated than ever.
  • The real challenge is to make productivity compatible with happiness.

🔹 Exemple de structure orale

“Work has always been both a source of pride and a source of pain.
As Karl Marx said, when people lose control over what they produce, they lose part of their humanity.
Today, we face another form of alienation: we are connected, monitored, and exhausted — in a world where productivity has become a religion.
The real question is not how much we work, but why we work.”
