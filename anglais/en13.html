🧩 T9 – Globalization

One world — but whose world?

🔹 Idée générale

La mondialisation a rapproché les peuples, ouvert les marchés et diffusé la culture.
Mais elle a aussi creusé les inégalités, uniformisé les modes de vie et fragilisé les identités locales.
L’enjeu : concilier ouverture et préservation.

🔹 Idées clés à développer

  1. Economic dimension : free trade, outsourcing, interdependence.
  2. Cultural dimension : Americanization, global brands, loss of diversity.
  3. Social inequalities : winners and losers of globalization.
  4. Environmental impact : transport, pollution, global supply chains.
  5. Political backlash : nationalism, populism, protectionism.

🔹 Citations intégrées dans des phrases

  • As Thomas Friedman wrote, “The world is flat,” meaning that technology and trade have erased borders — but not inequalities.
  • Joseph Stiglitz warned that “globalization has not lived up to its promise,” because it has served corporations more than citizens.
  • Zygmunt Bauman described our time as “liquid modernity,” where people and ideas flow everywhere but few find stability.
  • Fukuyama once believed that history had ended with democracy’s victory — yet globalization has shown that history always comes back with a vengeance.
  • As Gandhi famously said, “I do not want my house to be walled in on all sides, but I do not want the wind to blow away my roof either” — a perfect image of balanced globalization.

🔹 Exemples de phrases prêtes à placer

  • Globalization has made the world richer, but not fairer.
  • The same brands, the same shows, and the same apps are everywhere — diversity is becoming a luxury.
  • Outsourcing creates jobs abroad but unemployment at home.
  • Global trade connects us economically but divides us morally.
  • We have global markets but national emotions.

🔹 Exemple de structure orale

“Globalization was supposed to unite humanity — but it often divides it.
As Joseph Stiglitz pointed out, it has lifted millions out of poverty while making billionaires richer.
The world is indeed flat, as Thomas Friedman said, yet for many people it still feels steep.
The challenge today is not to stop globalization, but to humanize it.”
