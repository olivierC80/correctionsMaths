🧩 T15 – Religion & Values

Faith, reason, and the search for meaning

🔹 Idée générale

Les religions ont longtemps structuré les sociétés et donné un sens à la vie collective.
Aujourd’hui, dans un monde sécularisé et pluraliste, elles coexistent avec la science, la liberté individuelle et la diversité culturelle.
L’enjeu : comment croire sans exclure, et penser sans mépriser ceux qui croient ?

🔹 Idées clés à développer

  1. Religion as foundation of values : moral compass, community, identity.
  2. Secularization : decline of traditional faith, rise of individual spirituality.
  3. Tolerance and pluralism : coexistence of beliefs in democratic societies.
  4. Conflicts and extremism : religion as a source of both peace and violence.
  5. Science vs faith : complementarity or opposition?

🔹 Citations intégrées dans des phrases

  • Voltaire, after the Calas affair, famously wrote his Treatise on Tolerance, reminding us that “it is better to risk saving a guilty man than to condemn an innocent one.”
  • Pascal observed that “the heart has its reasons which reason knows nothing of” — suggesting that faith answers needs that logic alone cannot satisfy.
  • John Locke defended freedom of belief, arguing that “no man can be forced to believe” — one of the foundations of modern secularism.
  • As Gandhi said, “My religion is based on truth and non-violence” — proof that faith can inspire moral strength rather than division.
  • Martin Luther King Jr. turned religion into action, declaring that “injustice anywhere is a threat to justice everywhere” — linking faith and ethics.

🔹 Exemples de phrases prêtes à placer

  • Faith can unite people through compassion — or divide them through dogma.
  • In secular societies, religion is no longer a rule, but a choice.
  • Science explains how the world works; religion asks why it exists.
  • Tolerance is not about agreeing with others, but about accepting their right to disagree.
  • When faith becomes politics, truth becomes a weapon.

🔹 Exemple de structure orale

“Religion once explained everything; today, it explains less, but it still means a lot.
As Pascal said, the heart has its reasons that reason ignores.
Voltaire and Locke fought for tolerance, because forcing belief destroys faith itself.
In our secular societies, the challenge is not to choose between faith and reason, but to let both coexist in respect and freedom.”
