🧩 T10 – Inequality

A fair world — or a world for the few?

🔹 Idée générale

L’inégalité n’est pas nouvelle, mais elle prend aujourd’hui des formes économiques, sociales, éducatives et géographiques inédites.
Elle interroge la justice, la méritocratie et le contrat social : jusqu’où la société peut-elle tolérer l’écart entre les riches et les pauvres ?

🔹 Idées clés à développer

  1. Economic inequality : wealth concentration, top 1%, working poor.
  2. Educational inequality : access to schools, digital divide, merit vs privilege.
  3. Gender and racial inequality : persistent gaps and systemic barriers.
  4. Global inequality : North vs South, labor exploitation.
  5. Solutions : redistribution, taxation, equal opportunities, universal basic income.

🔹 Citations intégrées dans des phrases

  • As Thomas Piketty showed, “When the rate of return on capital exceeds the rate of growth, inequality automatically increases.”
  • John Rawls imagined a “veil of ignorance” — a society would only be just if people accepted its rules without knowing whether they would be rich or poor.
  • Nelson Mandela reminded the world that “overcoming poverty is not a gesture of charity; it is an act of justice.”
  • Martin Luther King Jr. warned that “injustice anywhere is a threat to justice everywhere” — a timeless truth in an interconnected world.
  • In The Great Gatsby, Fitzgerald exposed the myth of meritocracy: success is often inherited, not earned.

🔹 Exemples de phrases prêtes à placer

  • We live in a time where billionaires go to space while millions can’t afford healthcare.
  • Inequality is not only about money — it’s about dignity, access, and opportunity.
  • Social mobility has become the exception rather than the rule.
  • Redistribution is not about punishing success, but about giving everyone a fair start.
  • A society that tolerates extreme inequality risks losing both justice and stability.
