🧩 T8 – Science & Ethics

Can we, or should we?

🔹 Idée générale

La science a transformé notre monde — médecine, technologie, intelligence artificielle, génétique.
Mais chaque progrès soulève une question éthique : jusqu’où aller ?
Le dilemme moderne n’est plus seulement can we do it? mais should we do it?

🔹 Idées clés à développer

  1. Progress and responsibility : the double-edged nature of scientific discovery.
  2. Genetics and bioethics : cloning, gene editing, CRISPR, eugenics.
  3. Artificial intelligence : autonomy, privacy, replacement of human judgment.
  4. Environmental ethics : science as saviour or destroyer of the planet.
  5. The scientist’s role : moral duty, humility, and the limits of knowledge.

🔹 Citations intégrées dans des phrases

  • As Mary Shelley warned in Frankenstein, when science forgets morality, its creator can quickly become its victim.
  • Oppenheimer famously quoted the Bhagavad Gita — “Now I am become Death, the destroyer of worlds” — when he realized the horror of his own success.
  • Isaac Asimov imagined that robots would one day need “laws of robotics” — rules to protect humans from their own creations.
  • Stephen Hawking once said, “The development of full artificial intelligence could spell the end of the human race” — a reminder that not all progress is progress.
  • As Einstein wrote, “Science without religion is lame, religion without science is blind” — meaning that reason and ethics must work together.

🔹 Exemples de phrases prêtes à placer

  • Scientific progress should serve humanity, not replace it.
  • The border between innovation and manipulation is getting thinner every year.
  • With great power comes great responsibility — especially in science.
  • AI doesn’t need to hate us to destroy us; it just needs to follow our orders too well.
  • We are not gods — and forgetting this is the oldest human mistake.

🔹 Exemple de structure orale

“Science gives us power — but not necessarily wisdom.
As Mary Shelley showed in Frankenstein, the scientist who creates life becomes enslaved by it.
From Oppenheimer’s remorse to CRISPR’s promises, every discovery reminds us that knowledge without ethics can be dangerous.
The question is no longer what we can do, but what we should dare to do.”
