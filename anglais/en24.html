🎬 Blade Runner (Ridley Scott, 1982)

Inspired by Philip K. Dick’s novel Do Androids Dream of Electric Sheep?

🧠 T1 – Technology & Society

Angle : L’humanité face à ses créations : progrès technologique, déshumanisation, peur du remplacement.

The film imagines a future where humans have created “replicants,” artificial beings almost indistinguishable from people. Technology meant to serve humanity becomes its mirror — and its rival. It questions what remains human when everything can be reproduced, even emotions.

Phrase-type : As Ridley Scott shows in Blade Runner, our biggest fear is not that machines will kill us, but that they will become us.

⚙️ T8 – Science & Ethics

Angle : L’éthique de la création : peut-on fabriquer la vie sans en assumer la responsabilité ?

Scène clé : Roy Batty, le réplicant, tue son créateur (le Dr Tyrell) dans un geste symbolique : la créature se retourne contre son dieu.

Like Frankenstein, Blade Runner explores the limits of scientific creation. The replicants demand more life — a moral question, not a technical one. Science has reached the power of God but lost its sense of responsibility.

Phrase-type : The film is a moral warning: when science creates life, it also creates suffering.

🌍 T2 – Environment (secondaire)

Angle : Un futur écologiquement et moralement pollué : la Terre sombre sous la pluie acide, les villes sont étouffantes.

Blade Runner’s dark, polluted Los Angeles reflects both environmental decay and moral collapse. The visual atmosphere is a metaphor for our own environmental blindness.

Phrase-type : In Scott’s future, we have conquered technology but lost the planet.

🧬 T10 – Inequality (en filigrane)

Angle : Les réplicants sont des esclaves créés pour servir les humains — un parallèle avec les classes dominées.

The replicants symbolize modern inequality: created to work, denied freedom, and destroyed when they rebel. It’s a powerful metaphor for dehumanized labor in capitalist societies.

Phrase-type : The replicants are today’s working class — strong, efficient, and disposable.

🎨 T14 – Art & Culture

Angle : Une œuvre hybride : film noir + science-fiction + méditation philosophique.

Thématique : Qu’est-ce qui définit l’âme humaine ? La mémoire ? Les émotions ? La mort ?

Blade Runner is more than science fiction — it’s visual poetry. The famous “Tears in rain” monologue by Roy Batty is one of cinema’s most moving reflections on life and mortality.

Citation clé : “All those moments will be lost in time, like tears in rain.”

Phrase-type : The replicant dies more humanly than the humans — that’s the paradox at the heart of the film.

🧩 T11 – Media & Truth (option secondaire)

Angle : Identité et illusion : dans un monde saturé d’images, comment distinguer le réel de l’artificiel ?

The film anticipates our post-truth society: reality is artificial, memories can be manufactured, and perception is manipulated. Like deepfakes today, Blade Runner warns that truth can be fabricated.

💬 À retenir pour impressionner le jury

“Blade Runner turns science fiction into a moral mirror. It asks a timeless question: what makes us truly human — biology, memory, or empathy?”
