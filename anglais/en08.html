🧩 T4 – Gender Equality

Beyond equality: towards real empowerment

🔹 Idée générale

Le thème de l’égalité hommes-femmes touche à la justice, la liberté, et la reconnaissance.
Les progrès sont réels, mais les stéréotypes, les inégalités salariales, et les violences persistent.
L’enjeu : comment passer de l’égalité juridique à l’égalité réelle ?

🔹 Idées clés à développer

  1. Historical fight for rights : suffragettes, feminism, equal pay.
  2. Workplace equality : glass ceiling, pay gap, leadership positions.
  3. Cultural stereotypes : education, media, family roles.
  4. Intersectionality : gender + race + class.
  5. Current debates : gender identity, toxic masculinity, balance between equality and difference.

🔹 Citations intégrées dans des phrases

  • As Simone de Beauvoir famously wrote, “One is not born, but rather becomes, a woman,” which reminds us that gender inequality is socially constructed, not biological.
  • Virginia Woolf insisted that women need “a room of one’s own” — independence, both material and intellectual, to create and to think freely.
  • Nelson Mandela once said that freedom cannot be achieved unless women have been emancipated from all forms of oppression — a truth that still resonates today.
  • When Malala Yousafzai declared that “one child, one teacher, one book, and one pen can change the world,” she made education a feminist cause.
  • As Emma Watson said at the UN, “Gender equality is your issue too” — equality is not a women’s battle but a human one.

🔹 Exemples de phrases prêtes à placer

  • We have achieved equality in law, but not always in life.
  • The glass ceiling may be invisible, but it is still very real.
  • Gender equality benefits everyone: societies that empower women grow faster and fairer.
  • Social media have given women a voice, but also exposed them to new forms of harassment.
  • Men must be allies, not spectators, in the fight for equality.

🔹 Exemple de structure pour une colle

“Gender equality has made progress, but we still live in a world where power often has a male face.
As Simone de Beauvoir said, women become who they are through education and social norms — which means that true equality starts at school.
Today, movements like #MeToo and HeForShe show that change is possible, but also that men and women must fight it together.”
